Elo Distribution

faction win/loss, scaled by role-relative performance across every battle report — click a player to see their classes and a battle-by-battle stat log under that name
How is this calculated? (the Elo → price formula)
Elo. Everyone starts at 1500. After each battle report:
  • Your faction's result that battle is whichever side had fewer total deaths (a tie on deaths is a draw). In a battle with more than two factions, there's no single win/loss — instead every player is compared head-to-head against every other player in the report using individual performance (below), and rated against all of them at once.
  • Your rating moves by the standard chess-style Elo formula: how much your team over- or under-performed what your Elo, versus the enemy team's average Elo, predicted.
  • How far a single battle can move you shrinks as you play more: up to 40 points for your first 9 battles, 24 up to your 30th, 16 after that — so new players settle in fast and veterans move gradually.
  • Your own performance that battle scales the swing up or down by up to 40% either way. It's role-relative: each of damage, healing, kills, assists and deaths is compared to other players in the same class in that same battle, not the whole field — so a Medic is judged mostly on healing/deaths/assists, everyone else mostly on damage/deaths/assists. Playing well cushions a loss; playing badly shrinks a win.
  • A lopsided result (a much bigger death-count gap between the two sides) moves ratings up to 50% more than a close one.
Price. After every report, your price is recomputed fresh from where your Elo sits relative to every other currently-tracked player — specifically, how many standard deviations above or below the average Elo you are right now. That's converted to a price on a straight line: dead-average Elo prices at $100, and 3 standard deviations above average prices at $10,000 (the ceiling — Elo further above that doesn't add more). Below average, price falls the same way each standard deviation, down to a floor of $0.01 — since the line is straight and floored rather than curved, it's possible for a fair number of players to end up bunched at that floor if the market's Elo spread is narrow relative to the median/top gap. This whole formula recomputes for everyone whenever new reports come in or an admin changes the price-scale settings, so your price reflects your standing in the current field, not just your own history.
